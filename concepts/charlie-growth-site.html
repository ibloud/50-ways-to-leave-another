Work for trade

Trade value—not ownership by accident.

A fair exchange can help independent artists obtain professional services without pretending labor is free. It needs the same clarity as a cash commission.

Example framework · not an offer

Website system ↔ agreed creative services

Loptr Lab contributes
A defined site design/build package, documentation and handoff valued in writing.
Artist contributes
Specific writing, performance or production deliverables valued in writing.
Balance
If values differ, cash, reduced scope or staged credits reconcile the difference.
Acceptance
Each side approves objective deliverables and receives a correction period.

What each party keeps

  • All pre-existing music, code, brands, methods and materials
  • General skills, know-how and reusable tools
  • Unrelated catalog, audience and future work

What must be separately agreed

  • Ownership or license for the finished site
  • Master ownership and recording-use permissions
  • Songwriting and publishing splits based on actual authorship
  • Credit, portfolio display, exclusivity and promotion

What trade does not mean

  • No payment “in exposure”
  • No blanket transfer of catalog, publishing, voice or likeness
  • No perpetual exclusivity unless specifically valued and accepted
  • No AI training on private work or identity without separate consent
Write it down before work begins. List fair-market values, tax responsibility, milestones, licenses, delivery, cancellation and what happens if only one side completes. A trade credit pays for defined services; it does not automatically purchase copyright. Song splits should reflect actual composition, and master rights should be stated independently. Obtain qualified legal and tax advice where the stakes justify it.

Plain-language working terms

Five questions before “yes.”

  1. 01

    What are we making?

    Song, verse, production, mix or coordinated project—with formats and deadline.

  2. 02

    Who wrote what?

    Record actual lyric, melody and composition contributions on a split sheet.

  3. 03

    Who owns the master?

    State ownership or license, payment condition, term, territory and permitted uses.

  4. 04

    What needs clearance?

    Identify beats, samples, stems, interpolations, collaborators and AI-assisted material.

  5. 05

    What happens if plans change?

    Use revision limits, cure periods, cancellation terms and delivery of completed work.
