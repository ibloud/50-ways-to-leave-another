# Distribution, payment and takedowns: the DistroKid research thread

**Source check: 19 October 2026 · Disputed allegations · Current court status not fully verified**

Who controls access to a released song, and what evidence lets an artist challenge a decision? This case note connects the existing platform-independence and rights work in *50 Ways to Leave Another* to distribution accountability. It does not determine liability or recommend a distributor.

## How Dominique arrived at this question

On 19 October 2026, Dominique Devereaux reported first noticing a Skinner Brothers Instagram post saying they had not been paid for streaming for months. Dominique did not investigate then; a later, independently encountered reference prompted research and discussion of DistroKid.

This records Dominique's observation and research sequence, not independent verification of the post or the payment claim. The original post URL, publication date, exact wording, account records, amounts, contractual payment dates and distributor response have not been retrieved. Search results did not locate the post. We do not infer that the band is a party to the UMG case, that its concern arose from that case, or that any payment was unlawfully withheld. A second reference is a reason to investigate, not corroboration of the first claim.

The later video summary supplied by Dominique discussed copyright litigation, artist removals and streaming fraud. Its URL and full video were not supplied or independently reviewed. Its procedural advice is therefore checked against official guidance below rather than repeated as a blanket recommendation.

AI-assisted creation, infringement, manipulated listening counts, removal and payment delays are distinct questions. Evidence of one does not establish another. Reported artist removals also do not by themselves establish why a particular track disappeared.

## Decisions for a balanced discussion

| Decision | Relevant principle | Limit of the comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Lenz v. Universal Music, Ninth Circuit, amended 2016](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2016/03/17/13-16106.pdf) | A copyright claimant must consider fair use before issuing a DMCA notice; the court applies a subjective good-faith standard. | An incorrect notice does not automatically establish liability. This does not decide payment or fraud-policy disputes. |
| [Viacom v. YouTube, Second Circuit, 2012](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/10-3270/10-3270-2012-04-05.html) | Specific knowledge and willful blindness matter to the section 512(c) safe-harbor analysis; general awareness of infringement is insufficient by itself. | The court vacated the summary judgment and remanded. A hosting platform's circumstances cannot simply be assigned to a music distributor. |
| [MGM v. Grokster, Supreme Court, 2005](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/04-480) | Distribution of a product with the object of promoting infringement, shown by clear expression or affirmative steps, can support inducement liability. | Lawful uses do not excuse inducement, but infringement by users alone does not establish inducement by the provider. |

These decisions address different facts and theories. They frame questions about enforcement, knowledge and conduct; they do not resolve UMG's allegations or establish a general rule about AI music.

## Identify the problem before choosing a response

| Problem reported | Evidence to preserve | Appropriate starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copyright removal | Notice, claimant, track/version identifiers, licenses, contribution records and correspondence | [DistroKid's official DMCA guidance](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/360056369674-DMCA-Takedowns-and-Counter-Notices) describes counter-notices for disputed copyright removals. Review the actual notice and legal consequences before submitting. |
| Artificial-streaming or other policy action | Stated policy, platform notice, listening reports, promotion purchases and support history | Use the identified platform/distributor review process. [DistroKid explains artificial streaming here](https://support.distrokid.com/hc/en-us/articles/360013647373-What-is-Artificial-Streaming). A DMCA counter-notice is not a universal appeal. |
| Missing or delayed payment | Reporting periods, statements, dashboard estimates, withdrawal history, contractual dates, deductions/holds and ticket references | Request an account-specific reconciliation through official support. Separate estimated earnings, reported royalties, available balance and received cash. Do not infer a copyright claim or fraud finding from delay alone. |

Counter-notices involve legal declarations, disclosure of contact information and possible litigation. See [17 U.S.C. section 512(g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512). This page does not prepare a counter-notice or claim that removal must be reversed. Keep statements, contracts and personal information outside the public repository.

## Original fictional exercise: a release becomes unavailable

A fictional artist sees a track disappear and an expected payment fail to arrive. The reason is unknown. Do not assign these events to the Skinner Brothers or any real artist.

1. Identify each separate question: availability, copyright, platform policy, reporting and payment.
2. List what the notice actually says, what the artist reports, and what remains unknown.
3. Match the track/version to its permissions, contributors and delivery metadata.
4. Prepare a factual support request asking for the decision basis, affected releases, reporting periods and review route. Do not submit it as part of the exercise.
5. Explain what evidence would change your assessment in favor of the artist, rights holder or distributor.
6. Preserve the canonical project page and lawful source archive without re-uploading disputed material to evade enforcement.
