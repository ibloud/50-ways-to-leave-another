## Separate the rights questions

**Legal principle — two works.** The composition and the recorded performance are separate copyright subjects (Copyright Office Circular 56A, pages 1–2). Establish each ownership record independently; being the named performer does not prove ownership of either. Check writers, performers, producers, publishers, labels and transfers rather than assigning all rights to Ren.

**Legal principle — authorship.** The Copyright Office's January 2025 Part 2 report, sections II.C–F, distinguishes assistance from generation. Human expression retained in the result, original modifications and sufficiently creative selection or arrangement may qualify; wholly generated material does not. Prompts alone generally do not supply sufficient control under the technology assessed. Creative direction, effort or paying for a service does not itself establish authorship. Inventory actual contributions and disclose/exclude unprotectable AI material as appropriate in registration; commercial permission is not registration eligibility.

**Legal principle — authorization.** Title 17 sections 101–103 and 106 distinguish adaptation rights from protection for new expression. Owning a new contribution does not transfer the underlying work or authorize its use. Sections 201 and 204 address initial ownership and signed transfers; a collaboration label alone does not establish joint authorship or execute a transfer. Record work-for-hire questions for counsel.

**Interpretation — style and copying.** General production ideas are distinct from protected expression (section 102(b)). A style reference is not blanket clearance: inspect any retained melody, lyrics, arrangement or recorded sounds. Sampling implicates recording and composition permissions; interpolation can implicate composition rights without copying a master. Section 114(b)'s limits for independently fixed sounds do not clear composition copying. Identifiable voice imitation also raises separate consent, publicity, digital-replica and misleading-endorsement questions. Refer jurisdiction-specific issues to counsel; this case supplies no imitation or fair-use determination.

**Interpretation — publication boundary.** Discussing these questions publicly does not authorize redistributing or processing the source recording. Keep protected evidence and contracts in a private index. Credit is not permission; permission is not ownership; ownership shares, royalty shares and collection arrangements are separate records.

## Platform comparison

**Verified platform provisions, checked November 9, 2026:** Suno terms revised August 10, effective September 3, 2026, remain displayed. Under “Pro and Premier Accounts,” assignment covers Suno-owned Output from the subscriber's Submissions, subject to terms; copyright vesting is not warranted. Basic output is personal/noncommercial. “Permitted Commercial Use” requires an approved-channel Download within plan allocations and compliant use; download rights survive later subscription changes. Enabled third-party Remixes are contractually joint/equal only insofar as copyright vests, and personal/noncommercial regardless of tier, absent a separate offering. These provisions do not clear third-party inputs. “Using the Service” requires submission authority, including the platform's contemplated processing/model uses.

**Verified help guidance:** “Who owns an extension?” (edited January 7, 2026) warns against assuming ownership or monetizing another person's extension, or treating a Basic-origin song as cleared through extension. Its shorthand about owning paid outputs is not a guarantee of statutory copyright. The Remix FAQ (edited May 22, 2025) groups Cover, Extend, Reuse and Speed as Remix workflows. It distinguishes another user's song (noncommercial) from one's own paid-origin song remixed while subscribed (commercial eligibility). Apply the later terms and exact feature facts; not every extension is the enabled third-party Remix arrangement. Escalate conflicts or separate-offering claims before use.

**Unresolved:** composition holder unknown; master holder unknown. Chain map: publicly associated recording → unidentified contributors/transfers → unknown holders → no documented processing/adaptation license. No signed permissions or account/feature records supplied. Human/AI inventory is proposed only: creator's brief, ChatGPT assistance and potential Suno output; no actual retained expression or output is available to assess.

**Verified platform provision:** use the dated comparison above, not a claim that an account was used. **Proposal:** negotiate credit and revenue separately after verifying contributors, ownership and permissions; shares remain unagreed. **Register:** producer obtains a private evidence index for both chains; counsel reviews processing/adaptation scope and platform compatibility before any upload. No deadline is invented.

**Decision — HOLD** source processing, continuation and commercial release. Request signed authority from verified holders covering the intended use, contributors' permissions, any required sample/interpolation and voice consents, applicable platform/plan/Download evidence and review of the actual result. A collaboration agreement cannot override platform restrictions by itself. **Choose another option:** create independent music with original or authorized inputs and reassess it.
