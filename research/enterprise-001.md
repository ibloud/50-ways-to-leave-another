## Fictional facts and the weak assumption

A disabled founder in Minnesota proposes an accessibility-first creative enterprise, a $1 monthly founder payment and paid collaborators. A practice ledger estimates $3,000 monthly customer receipts, $1,200 collaborator costs and $400 operating costs. All numbers are fictional proposals, not actual income, allowed deductions or benefit calculations. The founder proposes 90 hours of monthly work. Whether the founder receives SSI, SSDI, both or a particular housing subsidy is deliberately unspecified.

**Interpretation:** the $1 label cannot answer the reporting question. Ownership, work performed, net earnings, distributions, business assets and program-specific rules must be examined separately. The $1,400 arithmetic remainder is not automatically countable SSI income, SSDI earnings, HUD income, disposable cash or a distribution. Do not use nominal pay, hidden distributions, proxy ownership, sham transfers or unreported income to evade rules.

## SSI and SSDI are separate evaluations

**Verified authority/guidance — U.S. federal; checked November 9, 2026.** Sources SSA-01 through SSA-08 in the [register](https://ibloud.github.io/legal-rights/sources.html) give URLs, editions, effective periods and limitations.

**SSDI:** in 2026, monthly earnings **exceeding $1,210** can count as a trial-work service month; nine service months can occur within a rolling 60 months. For self-employment, **more than 80 hours in a month** is a separate service trigger under 20 CFR 404.1592(b)(2). This is not a universal income ceiling. The fictional 90-hour proposal warrants review even with $1 pay. Eligibility, previous trial months, later substantial-gainful-activity analysis, extended eligibility and reinstatement depend on facts. POMS DI 10510.010 distinguishes three general self-employment tests from the countable-income test when applicable; hours, services, value and income cannot be reduced to salary alone.

**SSI:** no SSDI trial work period applies. Wages, net earnings from self-employment, other income and countable resources require separate records and applicable exclusions. SSA's property-essential-to-self-support guidance identifies possible business-property exclusions; that does not establish exclusion for every asset or under HUD. PASS requires a qualifying written work goal and SSA approval; do not preemptively exclude money merely because it is earmarked for a business. Review applicable expense exclusions and continued-health-coverage supports with a benefits counselor. No personal payment amount is calculated here.

**Private next step:** verify the person's actual benefits and work history through their authorized records, keep work-hours and business ledgers, and ask SSA/independent benefits counseling how and when to report. [SSA WIPA](https://www.ssa.gov/work/WIPA.html) and [Minnesota DB101](https://mn.db101.org/) are review resources; DB101 is education, not binding authority. Do not post benefit letters or identifiers in GitHub.

## Housing is a third calculation

**Verified federal text/guidance — checked November 9, 2026:** HUD-01/02 link 24 CFR 5.609 and 5.603 (eCFR displayed through November 5, 2026). Section 5.609(b)(28) distinguishes gross business receipts from net income and withdrawals, limits expansion/capital-debt deductions and addresses investment reimbursement. A tax deduction is not automatically a HUD deduction. Net-family-asset treatment, transfers below fair value and income from assets are separate questions. Section 5.609(c) distinguishes initial/interim and annual income calculations.

**Implementation limits:** PIH Notice 2026-15, issued May 14, 2026, generally sets January 1, 2027 enforcement with MTW/FRS exceptions and preserves selected earlier requirements. Multifamily Notice H2025-07, issued December 17, 2025, sets January 1, 2027 compliance for covered owners. Neither establishes the fictional founder's applicable program, property's conversion date or local reporting deadline.

**Unresolved:** no lease, subsidy program, current PHA administrative plan, owner tenant-selection plan or recertification notice is supplied. Verify the property's program and applicable local rules privately with the administering PHA/owner; obtain written reporting and annual/interim recertification instructions. No Minneapolis housing eligibility or rent result is asserted. Do not postpone required reporting while waiting for this exercise or rely on the absence of a public answer as permission.

## Disability-centered hiring and fair work

**Verified guidance:** EEOC EMP-01/02 distinguishes voluntary disability-focused affirmative action from prohibited disability discrimination. Invitations to self-identify for a qualifying affirmative-action effort must explain purpose, voluntariness, no adverse treatment for refusal and confidentiality; keep that information separate from the application. Use accessible recruitment, essential job functions and reasonable-accommodation processes. Do not demand diagnoses in public applications, pay disabled workers less because of disability, or use an unpaid label to bypass employee protections. Federal coverage and state/local duties require review of the actual employer and role.

**Verified rates — effective January 1, 2026; checked November 9, 2026:** Minnesota DLI lists **$11.41/hour**; Minneapolis lists **$16.37/hour** for covered work physically performed there (EMP-03/04). These are legal reference figures, not sample compensation proposals. Check the current date, location, coverage, overtime, paid leave/sick time, wage notices, recordkeeping and payroll before hiring. No $1 employee-pay exception is inferred; the founder's classification is unresolved.

## People over profit: a reviewable proposal

Budget maintenance and accessibility labor. Agree exit, termination and succession without silently taking a departing contributor's rights. This is a proposed governance checklist, not nonprofit status, tax exemption or legal compliance.
