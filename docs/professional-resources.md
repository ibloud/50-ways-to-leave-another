# Professional resources and ecosystem relationships

This register distinguishes **external professional resources** from project-owned intellectual property and from actual collaborators or counterparties.

A resource can inform the project without becoming a project partner, endorser, contributor, licensor, or rights holder.

### [Thanks Mom — U.S. trademark clearance](https://www.thanksmom.ai/)

A paid service that describes its offering as AI-assisted trademark searches with attorney review and a clearance report. Relevant when considering a name, slogan, or logo before investing in a launch.

Listed as an external professional resource; service quality has not been independently evaluated by 50 Ways. No partnership, endorsement, or attorney-client relationship with the project is implied.

The provider states that its trademark assessment covers the United States only, its operator is not a law firm, and results cannot be guaranteed. Trademark clearance is separate from permission to use music, recordings, artwork, or other copyrighted material.

Before asking for action, identify whether the concern involves a recording agreement, publishing agreement, distribution arrangement or another relationship. Separate the artist's account, documents, established findings and unanswered questions. Keep contracts and identifying financial records private for qualified review. Do not describe an unfavorable agreement as fraud without supporting evidence.

### Questions to take to professionals and public officials

- What minimum royalty reporting should show deductions and recoupment balances?
- What access to records and affordable independent audits should creators have?
- How should duration, renewal, rights-return and dispute procedures be explained and reviewed?
- Which existing protections apply, what gap remains, and which jurisdiction or office can address it?

Use the same questions across offices. Record the source, date and exact scope of each public response; label no response as no response, not opposition. These questions do not endorse a party or candidate. No enquiries have been sent.

PIXIE can help organise a source list, timeline and questions for human review. Made-Sick remains the resource directory; 50 Ways supplies the creative and educational context. ## Integration rule

Professional resources are treated as **named external nodes** in the project architecture.

They may supply:

- education and domain expertise;
- public reference material;
- frameworks that inform project controls;
- potential speakers, instructors, advisors, or programming partners;
- potential licensing or distribution relationships.

They do **not** automatically supply:

- project ownership;
- endorsement;
- authorship;
- permission to reproduce;
- permission to use a person's name, likeness, voice, logo, course files, recordings, or proprietary materials;
- an attorney-client relationship;
- a contractual partnership.

## Programming relationship

When a professional resource becomes relevant to programming, create a project-owned program record that identifies:

1. the external resource and its public-source basis;
2. the original project interpretation or application;
3. the proposed format;
4. the rights/permission state;
5. the commercial relationship contemplated, if any;
6. the event and recording implications.

This allows the same professional resource to be represented consistently across legal, business, curriculum, web, and live-event planning.

## Status vocabulary

- **Professional resource** — external expertise or ecosystem the project may learn from or reference.
- **Reference** — source used for a documented claim or project-control idea.
- **Prospective collaborator** — a person or organization the project may approach; no participation is implied.
- **Partner** — use only after an actual agreement establishes that relationship.
- **Endorser** — use only with explicit permission or documented public endorsement.
- **Rights holder / licensor** — use only when the relevant rights and authority are documented.
