# 50 Ways to Leave Another: Independent Creator Guide

**Audience:** junior producers and independent creators reducing or ending dependence on YouTube

**Project status:** educational, pre-production, and proposal material; not a completed or cleared release

**Owner:** Dominique Devereaux / Loptr Lab

**Last reconciled:** September 22, 2026

This guide turns the repository and public site into one usable pathway. It teaches how to define a music-first project, preserve authorship, budget without counting imaginary income, prepare a release-ready handoff, and build an owned public presence. It is educational material—not legal, tax, accounting, investment, safeguarding, or employment advice.

## Read the labels first

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **FACT** | Supported by a current project control file or linked authoritative/public source. Facts with changing terms must be rechecked before use. |
| **ESTIMATE** | A conservative planning range, not a promise or amount due. Replace it with the relevant contract, payer schedule, or professional advice. |
| **RECOMMENDATION** | A proposed project practice or decision. It is not an accepted agreement, attachment, clearance, or endorsement. |

## 1. Analyze the system before choosing platforms

The useful question is not “What replaces YouTube?” It is “What jobs was YouTube doing, and who should control each job now?” No single replacement should become the new single point of failure.

| Job | YouTube may have supplied | Independent arrangement | Classification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canonical identity | Channel and handle | Creator-controlled domain with stable biography, credits, and contact route | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| Media delivery | Video/audio hosting | Accessible host or storefront plus downloadable preservation copy | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| Discovery | Search, recommendations, shares | Search metadata, RSS/Atom, AT Protocol, newsletters, festivals, libraries, and aligned partners | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| Audience continuity | Subscribers and notifications | Consent-based mailing list and portable social identity | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| Conversation | Comments and community posts | Moderated forum, issue form, email, or an explicit no-comments policy | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| Proof of work | Public uploads and dates | Public portfolio, credits, version/provenance log, and protected source archive | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| Transactions | Ads, memberships, and platform tools | Commissions, tickets, grants, sponsorships, licensing, memberships, or storefronts selected separately | **RECOMMENDATION** |
| Direct-to-fan sales | Limited or indirect | Bandcamp says it connects fans directly with artists, notifies followers about releases, and currently returns an average of 82% of purchase revenue to artists after its share and processor fees | **FACT; recheck terms** |

### Analysis: use a portfolio of channels

**RECOMMENDATION:** Keep an owned site as the hub, protected storage as the source archive, and external services as replaceable spokes. A marketplace can be useful for discovery, reviews, scoped checkout, or payment protection without owning the creator’s identity. A storefront can prove released artistry without serving as a commissioning contract. A portable social protocol can aid discovery without becoming the rights ledger.

For every channel, answer these questions before relying on it:

1. What exact job does it perform?
2. Who owns the account, domain, contact list, analytics, and exported data?
3. What does the buyer pay and what does the creator actually receive?
4. Which files, revisions, credits, rights, territories, terms, and uses are included?
5. Can the archive, audience relationship, and proof of work leave with the creator?
6. What happens after suspension, closure, nonpayment, or a policy change?

**FACT — preservation boundary:** Moving away from a platform does not expand copyright, publicity, contractual, or music rights. Export only data the service permits; preserve titles, dates, URLs, captions, thumbnails, credits, licenses, and open-format copies; keep two backups in different locations; and verify the replacement before removing the old path. See [Platform Independence](platform-independence.md).

## 2. Build the minimum professional package

Work in this order so creative ambition does not outrun evidence or cash.

| Stage | Produce | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Define | One-page charter: audience, format, scope, roles, finish line, exclusions | A real person, brand, or platform is being treated as attached without evidence |
| Design | Track/episode map and creative brief | Third-party music, story, image, name, voice, or likeness is being treated as cleared |
| Organize | Folder map, naming/version rule, contribution log, two-backup check | Private evidence or credentials would enter the public repository |
| Budget | Cost budget, cash ledger, and survival/working/expansion cases | Contingent revenue is needed to pay an already-promised obligation |
| Protect | Rights envelope, split draft, consent record, AI record, risk list | Ownership, consent, youth safety, or intended-use rights remain unclear |
| Deliver | Masters/demos, alternate versions, stems, lyrics, credits, metadata, manifest, accessibility review | Required files, approvals, payments, licenses, or disclosures are missing |
| Publish | Accessible owned page plus deliberately selected external channels | The public page implies endorsement, clearance, or a commercial relationship that does not exist |

Before paid work begins, define parties, deliverables, milestones, revisions, acceptance, cancellation, payment, credit, confidentiality, AI use, ownership/licenses, subcontracting authority, and public use. Keep the chat transcript as supporting evidence, not the only operational record.

## 3. Treat cash timing as part of production

Do not confuse four different numbers:

| Item | Meaning | Planning treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | What the work is expected to cost | **ESTIMATE** and approve before commitment |
| Financing | Cash legally available for those costs | Count only executed, accessible funds |
| Revenue | Money the project may earn | Forecast by source, probability, restrictions, and timing |
| Cash receipt | Money cleared and available | Spend only after receipt, reconciliation, and restriction check |

### Operating waterfall for cash actually received

**RECOMMENDATION:** Apply each receipt in this order, subject to the controlling contracts and qualified advice:

1. Identify payer, project, track/episode, rights type, period, territory, currency, restrictions, and statement.
2. Remove tax or other pass-through amounts collected for someone else where applicable.
3. Reconcile platform, distributor, processor, collection, currency, and agent deductions against statements.
4. Fund tax and dispute reserves using advice appropriate to the actual person or entity.
5. Pay due contributor fees, milestones, reimbursements, and minimum guarantees.
6. Recoup only costs the signed agreement expressly makes recoupable.
7. Distribute master, composition, performer, producer, or backend shares from their separate ledgers.
8. Replenish operating runway for approved obligations.
9. Send only true surplus to the capital/community decision gate.

Never collapse composition, master, performer, and producer money into one undocumented pool.

### Realistic cash-timing context

The ranges below are planning assumptions. The payer’s current terms and the signed agreement control.

| Income source | What is known | Conservative planning treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commission or session work | Payment timing is contractual | **RECOMMENDATION:** require the funded deposit or first milestone before work; make the remainder due at acceptance or on a written net-15/net-30 invoice. Do not work beyond the funded milestone. |
| Up-front sync/master-use fee | Trigger depends on execution, delivery, approval, invoice, and use | **ESTIMATE:** model receipt 30–90 days after every required trigger, then replace the estimate with the negotiated contract. Interest or a pitch is worth $0 in the operating plan. |
| Distributor/master income | Reporting delays, reserves, thresholds, and chargebacks vary by DSP and distributor | **ESTIMATE:** use 60–120 days after the underlying activity for early planning unless the selected distributor publishes a different schedule; do not spend a dashboard estimate. |
| U.S. digital mechanicals | The MLC says U.S. DSPs send usage data and royalties monthly and it distributes matched royalties monthly | **FACT:** monthly cadence is not a guaranteed first-payment date. Registration and matching are prerequisites. **ESTIMATE:** allow 90–180 days from use for a first planning case, longer when data is unmatched or disputed. |
| SoundExchange-administered eligible uses | Direct-deposit accounts may receive monthly distributions after reaching $100; quarterly distribution thresholds are $10 for direct deposit and $100 for check | **FACT:** this applies only to SoundExchange-administered eligible uses, not every stream. Plan quarterly unless eligibility, registration, balance, and payment method support a faster case. |
| PRO performance income | Schedule depends on society, territory, reporting, cue sheets, and surveys | **ESTIMATE:** plan 6–12 months from a use for domestic receipts and longer for foreign collections until the actual society confirms otherwise. |
| Grant, sponsorship, or donation | Award, restriction, reporting, and disbursement documents control | **ESTIMATE:** allow 30–90 days after execution/conditions for planning, but commit nothing until funds are available. Keep restricted funds separate. |
| Proposed acquisition, co-production, or Netflix income | No agreement exists | **FACT:** record $0 in survival and working cases. |

### Use three cases

- **Survival case — FACT-based:** cash on hand plus executed near-term payments. Assume $0 royalties, sync, grants, sponsors, acquisitions, and Netflix money.
- **Working case — FACT plus conservative timing:** add signed milestone receivables and reported royalties supported by statements; include a late-payment reserve.
- **Expansion case — ESTIMATE:** model grants, licensing, programs, sponsorship, and co-production separately with probability, restrictions, decision date, and a no-funding response. Never use this case alone to justify an irreversible commitment.

Use the [cash-timing map](financial-waterfall-and-cash-timing.md) and [career capital worksheet](../templates/career-capital-allocation-model.md) to record evidence, trigger, contractual date, planning date, gross amount, deductions, restrictions, confidence, and follow-up owner.

## 4. Decide whether to buy tools or buy access

A camera, computer, studio build, software subscription, vehicle, storage system, or production suite can consume cash long after purchase. Depreciation may be an accounting treatment; it is not spendable cash.

| Path | Count before deciding | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy/own | Price, financing, tax, setup, insurance, storage, maintenance, software, accessibility, downtime, replacement, useful life, resale | Repeated high use, privacy/control needs, or reliable avoided rental cost |
| Rent/borrow | Rate, deposit, transport, availability, damage risk, setup time, support | Occasional or specialized work; uncertain demand |
| Maker/community/public-access space | Membership, booking, travel, accessibility, staff expertise, network, commercial-use rules, data/privacy limits | Learning, prototyping, supported production, shared equipment |
| Youth/community program | Eligibility, paid pathways, teaching capacity, consent, safeguarding, releases, insurance, transport, accessibility, ownership, restricted funding | Community benefit is a defined project outcome, not an afterthought |

**RECOMMENDATION:** Buy only when expected use, control, privacy, accessibility, or avoided rental cost justifies full lifecycle cost. Compare twelve-month and three-year cost, productive hours, cost per productive hour, maintenance labor, exit options, and what funded obligation would be delayed.

### Teen and young-adult learning pathways

Its public programs include Emerge Apprenticeships, the Summer No Comment Filmmaking Fellowship, professional learning, and other education and production pathways. ## 5. Require an LLM to push back responsibly

**FACT — project lesson:** On September 13, 2026, an AI system misread this project as only an EP archive and proposed removing intentional five-part-series and educational-resource functions. The proposed change was not merged. The correction became the [LLM Review and Pushback Protocol](../LLM-REVIEW-PROTOCOL.md).

The lesson is not that an LLM should never disagree. It should challenge unsupported claims and unsafe actions while also challenging its own first interpretation.

### Authority order

1. The creator’s current explicit instruction.
2. Current repository control files: status, provenance, AI disclosure, and rights/clearance.
3. Dated creator statements and approved briefs.
4. Code and Git history as evidence of implemented behavior.
5. Current authoritative external sources.
6. Model inference.

Memory is a navigation aid, not proof. If memory and current files disagree, the model must surface the conflict and check the source.

### When the model must stop and ask

Ask a focused clarifying question before deleting, reframing, or publishing when code appears deliberate but undocumented; site behavior and control files conflict; a real person, class, price, event, or relationship is unverified; “proposed” is ambiguous; several ecosystem artifacts are involved; memory conflicts with the repository; a whole public function would be removed; or authorship, licensing, endorsement, or model identity is inferred rather than evidenced.

**Good pushback example:** “The site contains dated external classes with automatic expiry, while the README foregrounds the EP. Are those classes intentional parts of the five-part development demonstration, or should the site be limited to the EP archive?”

**Required pre-edit record:** artifact; actual function; ecosystem relationship; current/proposed/unauthorized status; temporary behavior; third-party boundary; risk of change; sources checked; and a verification plan. If a material answer is missing, pause instead of inventing it.

## 7. Validate the service before scaling the facility

The teachable service is not “AI reads your paperwork for you.” It is a repeatable, accessibility-first method for helping a person understand a plan or notice they have already received.

**RECOMMENDATION — service definition:** Loptr Lab helps a participant identify the issuing organization and document purpose; separate quoted text from explanation; locate dates, decisions, responsibilities, and next actions; draft questions for the issuing professional; and verify the result against an authoritative source. The participant keeps decision-making authority.

This is education and document navigation—not legal, medical, financial, benefits, or vocational-rehabilitation advice. A group workshop should use public, fictional, or fully redacted examples and should not collect participants’ private plan documents. Any future individualized review needs a separate consent, privacy, retention, referral, and data-security process.

### Development provenance and employment-support boundary

**FACT — creator statement:** [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/) provided Dominique with one year of ChatGPT Plus access at an event. [SuperMe.ai](https://superme.ai/) made material development progress possible. Record these contributions as provenance and enabling support. Do not describe either organization as a sponsor, partner, endorser, fiscal sponsor, or source of matching funds without separate written confirmation.

**RECOMMENDATION — protected-record boundary:** Keep employment plans, disability or benefits information, case identifiers, budgets, and individualized support records outside the public repository and group workshop. The public-safe planning sequence is to test a virtual or community-based service before considering a capital-intensive facility. Neither phase should be described as approved or funded without written confirmation from the responsible organization.

**RECOMMENDATION — employment-plan use:** When a creator is already working with an authorized employment-support professional, provide that professional—not a public audience—with a short private crosswalk: task performed, customer, deliverable, hours, rate or funding source, accommodation, evidence produced, and next decision. Ask in writing whether the service fits the existing employment goal, whether a plan change is needed, which supports may be authorized, and what evidence is required. Do not count support until it is approved.

### Pilot sequence

| Stage | Site or stakeholder | Purpose | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enabling support | AARP and SuperMe.ai | Explain what enabled development | Provenance only; no sponsorship or partnership implied |
| Phase 0 | Whittier Co-op community room | Run a small resident trial in a familiar setting | **FACT — creator statement:** the room and Dusty property-management contact exist. Use is pending written approval; record actual time, cost, accessibility needs, and de-identified feedback. |
| Phase 1 | Dayton at Gaviidae YMCA | Test a repeatable version with a broader older-adult/community audience | **FACT — official public material:** the YMCA accepts Dayton community-room inquiries; its published flyer lists capacity 60 and a $250 two-hour rate. Availability, price, equipment, and any waiver must be reconfirmed. |
| Employment-plan review | Authorized employment-support professional | Connect the demonstrated work to an existing employment or business plan | Use private records, hours, deliverables, accommodation needs, and a revenue path; do not send participant documents. |
| Funding request | One eligible, reverified funder or fiscal sponsor | Request only the next evidence-backed step | The Drive file is a funding strategy, not a grant-ready proposal. Recheck the live opportunity, eligibility, deadline, award, permitted costs, match, reporting, and payment schedule. |

Whittier should come before a paid YMCA booking. A donated room may be reported as an in-kind contribution only after Dusty or the authorized property representative confirms the permission and value in writing. Until then, both room access and value are pending, not grant match.

### Keep the contact lanes separate

| Recipient | Send | Do not send |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dusty / Whittier property management | A public-safe request for a free resident education pilot; proposed date, duration, attendance cap, accessibility needs, no-sales/no-recording plan, and setup/cleanup needs | Private employment plans, disability or benefits records, case identifiers, participant documents, or housing recertification records |
| Dayton YMCA programming or event contact | One-page public workshop description, intended audience, room/AV/access needs, facilitator information, and requested fee or waiver | Employment-support or housing records, private participant examples, or claims of an AARP/SuperMe partnership |
| Authorized employment-support professional | Private plan crosswalk, business-plan update, service demonstration, actual hours/costs, assistive-technology needs, and questions requiring written approval | Unnecessary resident data; do not copy Dusty or YMCA unless the creator deliberately authorizes a specific introduction |
| Funder or fiscal sponsor | Reverified eligibility, approved pilot design, aggregate/de-identified results, actual budget, letters of venue support, and the next funded milestone | Unverified grant calendar claims, projected revenue presented as fact, private case material, or participant paperwork |

Ask Dusty to confirm resident eligibility, guest rules, capacity, tables/chairs, Wi-Fi, display and power, accessible entrance/seating/restroom, scheduling, setup and cleanup, insurance or waiver requirements, flyer approval, commercial/solicitation/data-collection restrictions, recording rules, and any fee or written waiver.

### Evidence the pilot should produce

- attendance and completion, without collecting unnecessary personal information;
- whether participants could correctly identify the issuer, purpose, deadline, question, and next action in a sample document;
- requested accommodations and unresolved access barriers;
- facilitator preparation, delivery, follow-up hours, and actual cost;
- public-safe feedback and permission status for any quotation;
- venue requirements, repeatability, and the smallest credible next budget; and
- a private employment-plan crosswalk plus a public, de-identified grant summary.

**RECOMMENDATION — grant gate:** Do not submit the broad funding strategy as an application. First produce a one-page pilot record, an actual cost table, a defined population and outcome, a privacy/safeguarding plan, written venue status, and a funder-specific eligibility check. Grant or sponsorship payment remains $0 in the survival and working cases until an award is executed and cash is available.

## 8. Final project handoff

The guide is complete only when another person can locate the work, understand the authority, verify the package, and see what remains blocked.

### Public-safe package

- [ ] Project charter and status use current/proposed/pending language accurately.
- [ ] Creative brief and track/episode map identify the intended work without exposing private source material.
- [ ] Public page links to status, provenance, AI disclosure, rights/clearance, and sources.
- [ ] Owned domain or canonical page is identified; outside platforms have one stated job each.
- [ ] Accessible text, captions/transcripts, alt text, keyboard access, and contact method are checked.
- [ ] Charlie pathway includes analysis, proposed upgrade/offer, and the proposed site referral with the non-endorsement boundary.
- [ ] Community-pilot claims separate creator statements, written venue and employment-support approvals, estimates, and recommendations.

### Protected production package

- [ ] Originals, preservation copies, sessions, stems, lyrics, metadata, and versions are organized.
- [ ] Contributor records, split sheets, rights envelopes, releases, contracts, invoices, receipts, and AI records are present.
- [ ] Two backups in different locations have been restored or spot-checked.
- [ ] Private evidence has stable locators but no public credentials or protected links.
- [ ] Budget-to-actual and cash ledgers distinguish estimates, receivables, reported amounts, and cleared cash.

### Finished-state check

You are finished with the educational package when the fictional or controlled capstone can be handed to a reviewer who can, without relying on chat memory:

1. identify what exists and what is only proposed;
2. locate every contributor, source, version, right, payment trigger, and unresolved issue;
3. reproduce the public-safe delivery from the protected archive;
4. explain how the creator can leave any external platform without losing the canonical identity or proof of work; and
5. stop the project at any missing consent, clearance, safeguarding, funding, or professional-review gate.

Passing this check does not mean the real EP or series is cleared, financed, commissioned, recorded, delivered, or released. It means the learner has produced a reviewable system that makes those next decisions possible.
