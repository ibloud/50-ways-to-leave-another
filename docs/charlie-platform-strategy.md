## 2. Bandcamp Friday — what it is and how Charlie should be using it

Bandcamp Friday is a recurring promotional event (typically the first Friday of each month) where
Bandcamp waives its revenue share entirely, passing 100% of sales directly to the artist.

**Current likely use:** Passive — profile exists, releases may be posted, no active promotion cycle.

**What active use looks like:**

### Release cadence
- Time instrumental releases, remix packs, and verse-only versions to land on Bandcamp Friday.
- A $5–$15 downloadable instrumental or a limited-edition verse stem pack released on Bandcamp
  Friday earns full margin with zero platform cut.
- Stack Bandcamp Friday drops with social pushes (TikTok, IG Reels, Bluesky) the Tuesday–Thursday
  prior so the algorithm has runway before the drop.

### Pricing architecture
| Product type              | Suggested price | Why                                                   |
|---------------------------|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Single instrumental       | $5–$10 name-your-price | Low friction, builds catalogue buyers          |
| Verse stem pack (3 takes) | $15–$25         | Producers pay for options — this is a real market     |
| Full EP (5 tracks)        | $7–$12          | Competitive with streaming, full margin               |
| Limited collab bundle     | $20–$50         | Scarcity + co-signed releases drive collector behavior|

### Fan relationship
Bandcamp is the only major platform where buyers follow artists, not algorithms. Every Bandcamp
Friday sale is also a list-building event. Charlie should treat each drop as a newsletter capture
moment, not just a revenue event.

### What this changes for the 50-ways-to-leave-another offer
A Charlie J who runs structured Bandcamp Friday drops is not just a freelance rapper — he is
an independent music business with a direct-to-fan revenue channel. That shifts the offer from
"pay per verse" toward "co-release with shared Bandcamp presence," which is materially more
valuable to both parties.

## 3. Sync licensing — current potential and gap analysis

### What sync licensing is
Synchronization (sync) licensing is the right to pair a composition and/or master recording with
moving image — trailers, games, ads, TV, film, branded content. It is one of the most durable
and high-margin revenue streams available to independent artists because:

- It pays upfront (sync fee) plus backend (performance royalties via PRO).
- It does not require streaming volume.
- It rewards distinctive voice and clear ownership documentation.

### Charlie's current sync potential

**Strengths:**
- UK rap vocal — a specific, identifiable sonic identity that sync supervisors can place. Generic
  vocals do not get placed; accents and character do.
- Multi-platform presence proves he is an active working artist, not a ghost profile — supervisors
  verify this.
- $200–$350 price point for session work demonstrates he can operate at indie-budget rates, which
  is the exact budget tier for most independent film, YouTube premium, and branded content work.

**Gaps:**
- No evidence of a public-facing sync-ready catalogue — stems, metadata, PRO registration, and
  ISRC codes are necessary before any supervisor can clear a track.
- No documented split sheet history. Sync supervisors will not place a track where ownership is
  ambiguous. Every verse Charlie records needs a co-writer agreement on file before it is pitched.
- No sync-specific metadata tagging. Bandcamp, AudioSalad, and DistroKid all support genre,
  mood, tempo, and instrumentation tags. These are the fields supervisors search.
- No demonstrated experience with brief-driven composition — sync work means writing to picture
  or to a creative brief, not free-form session delivery.

### What Charlie needs to do to grow this skill

**Stage 1 — Documentation (immediate, no cost)**
1. Register with a PRO (PRS for Music as a UK artist) if not already done.
2. Assign ISRCs to every released track via DistroKid, Amuse, or direct registration.
3. Create a split sheet for every past and future collaboration. Use a template like the one in
   `templates/split-sheet.md` in this repository.
4. Export stems for every completed track. Archive them in a controlled Drive folder with clear
   version history.

**Stage 2 — Catalogue preparation (30–90 days)**
1. Re-tag all Bandcamp releases with sync-relevant metadata: BPM, key, mood descriptors
   (e.g., "gritty," "urgent," "melancholic"), instrumentation, and vocal type.
2. Create 30-second and 60-second edits of existing tracks — standard brief lengths for ad and
   trailer placements.
3. Upload an instrumental version of every vocal track. Many sync placements use instrumental
   only, or swap between vocal and instrumental versions in post.

**Stage 3 — Pitching infrastructure (60–120 days)**
1. Build a one-page sync EPK: voice profile, genre, mood range, sample placements (even
   self-produced mockups), PRO affiliation, and split/clearance contact.
2. Submit to non-exclusive sync libraries: Musicbed, Artlist, Pond5, Soundsnap — these pay
   non-exclusive licensing fees and build placement history.
3. Target brief-driven platforms: Taxi, Matchmaker.fm, and Music Gateway all issue regular briefs
   from TV/film/ad buyers. Responding to briefs is the fastest way to build a sync track record.

**Stage 4 — Rate structure**
Once placement history exists, indie sync fees for a UK rap vocal in branded content typically
range from $250–$2,500 per placement at the budget tier. TV and film run higher. The ceiling is
not the skill gap — the documentation and discoverability gaps are.

## 4. Charlie as sync agent for the IBLoud IVXX Story Lab

### What the Story Lab needs

The `ibloud-ivxx-story-lab` repository documents a transmedia narrative project (IBLoud IVXX)
built around an original character, a distributed story arc across platforms, and a soundtrack
map that currently has significant clearance gaps. From the Story Lab's README:

> "A rights-aware catalogue incubator for replacing uncleared mashups with original compositions."

Tracks currently listed as "reference only" or "research reference pending provenance review"
need to be replaced with original, fully cleared compositions before the project can move to
commercial or licensed use. The Story Lab's CLEARANCE-LEDGER.md and TRACKLIST.md both document
this gap explicitly.

### What Charlie brings to that gap

Charlie's vocal profile — UK rap, distinctive accent, session-proven delivery — maps directly
to the Story Lab's narrative tone: urban, character-driven, textured. He is not a generic
session vocalist. His voice carries the "signal, witness, archive" quality that IBLoud IVXX
is built around.

Specifically:
- "iCall" (initiating signal) and "Nasty Game" (celebrity spectacle as conflict) are both
  narrative-function tracks where a distinctive vocal presence drives the story, not background
  texture.
- His AirGigs presence at $200/UK rap vocal positions him at exactly the budget tier the Story
  Lab can absorb for proof-of-concept replacements without requiring a full production budget.

### Sync agent framing — what this means practically

A sync agent relationship between Charlie and the Story Lab would work as follows:

| Role                   | Charlie J                                          | Loptr Lab / IBLoud IVXX Story Lab                   |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Creates                | Original vocal performances to replace uncleared tracks | Story arc, character, brief, and placement context |
| Owns                   | His master and composition share                   | The project framework, narrative IP, and sync pitch  |
| Earns                  | Session fee (upfront) + sync royalty share (backend) | Licensing revenue from Story Lab placements        |
| Documents              | Split sheet, ISRC, PRO registration                | CLEARANCE-LEDGER.md entries, rights envelope         |
| Controls               | Withdrawal/consent at any stage                    | Cannot place or represent his work without consent   |

This is not a work-for-hire arrangement. Charlie retains his composition share. Loptr Lab
functions as the sync agent: identifying placement opportunities, handling pitch infrastructure,
and splitting backend royalties according to the agreed split sheet.

### Immediate next steps if Charlie agrees

1. Agree on one track from the Story Lab TRACKLIST.md to replace with an original co-written
   and co-performed composition.
2. Execute a rights envelope (template in `templates/rights-envelope.md`) for that track before
   any recording begins.
3. Charlie registers the composition with PRS and assigns an ISRC.
4. Loptr Lab adds the track to CLEARANCE-LEDGER.md as `CLEARED — original composition,
   co-authored [Charlie J / Dominique Devereaux], split sheet on file`.
5. The track is released on Bandcamp (including on a Bandcamp Friday) with sync metadata tags,
   making it simultaneously a direct-to-fan release and a sync-ready asset.

That single track serves as proof of concept for the full catalogue replacement strategy and
demonstrates Charlie's sync viability with a documented, published placement.
