# Creative Brief Addendum — Track-by-Track Artist Brief
## 50 Ways to Leave Another · Loptr Lab / Dominique Devereaux
### For: Lead artist and production coordinator
### Format: Feelings-not-functions (per John August / The Nines methodology)

*This brief does not specify BPM, key, or production style. It describes what each track must feel like, what it must do in the scene it anchors, and what it must leave room for. Production decisions belong to the lead artist and coordinator, within these parameters.*

---

## How to Read This Brief

Each track has four fields:

- **Emotional texture** — what the song feels like from the inside. Not the subject matter — the feeling of inhabiting it.
- **Scene function** — what the song is doing in the episode it anchors. Not the plot — the work the music performs.
- **Lyrical constraints** — what the lyric must and must not do for the song to remain placeable across multiple scene uses.
- **Arrangement requirement** — one non-negotiable production specification per track, for sync and editorial usability.

---

## Track 1 — *50 Ways to Leave Another* (Title Track)

**Emotional texture:**
The morning after a decision that cannot be undone. Not grief — the period before grief, when you are still operating on momentum. Something that feels like calm but isn't. Broken clockwork that still tells accurate time.

**Scene function:**
Episode 1: heard as a working demo — rough, incomplete, the song before it knows what it is. Episode 5: the same song, finished, final, played in full. The production difference between these two versions is the arc of the entire series. The Episode 1 version should feel like a sketch; the Episode 5 version should feel like the answer to a question the first version was asking.

**Lyrical constraints:**
No names. No locations. No dates. First person throughout. The "you" being addressed should be ambiguous — it could be the person left, it could be the protagonist's former self. The lyric must carry the same weight whether heard in a scene about physical departure, emotional recurrence, or final resolution.

**Arrangement requirement:**
The intro must work under dialogue — 15 seconds minimum before the vocal enters, or a version with delayed vocal available. Modular sections: intro, verse, chorus each usable as standalone cues. Full stems required.

## Track 2 — *[Title TBD]*

**Emotional texture:**
Grief that presents as organizing. The feeling of sorting through objects in an empty room and realizing each one is a decision you made when you were someone slightly different. Not sadness — inventory. A kind of forensic tenderness.

**Scene function:**
Nova is reclaiming the apartment. The song scores the act of sorting: what was hers before him, what she changed to accommodate him, what she kept because she genuinely wanted it and what she kept because letting it go felt like admitting something. The song should make ordinary objects feel consequential without telling us which objects they are.

**Lyrical constraints:**
The lyric must avoid naming what is being sorted through. The specific detail belongs to the visual — the song provides the emotional frame. Universal enough that any listener's inventory plays in their mind alongside Nova's. Personal enough that it doesn't feel generic.

**Arrangement requirement:**
A natural pause point — a breath, a held note, a moment of silence — somewhere in the second half of the song. This is where the cut will happen when the scene needs it. Build it in; don't leave the editor to find one.

## Track 3 — *[Title TBD]*

**Emotional texture:**
The specific quality of dysregulation that looks, from the outside, like nothing. The inside of a brain that has lost its scaffolding and is continuing to function anyway, at reduced capacity, at cost. Not a breakdown — the feeling right before a breakdown that never quite arrives. Exhaustion performing competence.

**Scene function:**
Nova falls apart for three days after Dean calls. This is the episode where her neurodivergence is most directly present. The song is recorded in pieces — fragments, separate takes across three days — and assembled. The edit is the performance. The track should sound like it was made this way even if it wasn't. Seams are acceptable. Seams may be necessary.

**Lyrical constraints:**
Fractured syntax. Incomplete thoughts. Lines that start and redirect. The arrangement should mirror this — the lyric and production reinforce each other's instability. This is the one track where formal coherence is the wrong goal. It resolves, but not cleanly.

**Arrangement requirement:**
An unresolved chord at the end — or a resolution that arrives too late, after the listener has already accepted the unresolved state. No tidy ending. The supervisor will use this under the scene's final image, which also doesn't resolve.

## Track 4 — *[Title TBD]*

**Emotional texture:**
The experience of being seen by someone who is not trying to explain you. Not being understood — being witnessed. These are different. Understanding requires a framework. Witnessing just requires presence. The song should feel like the second one.

**Scene function:**
Charlie performs this track. Nova watches through the glass. Mama D's voice (a hummed melody, a few bars) is embedded in the bridge — processed, woven into the production. The song is about being recognized across distance: generational distance, creative distance, the distance between what you make and what it means to someone else. Nova does not perform on this one. She is the audience.

**Lyrical constraints:**
Second person — but the "you" is genuinely ambiguous. It could be addressed to Nova, to Mama D, to the listener, to the protagonist talking to herself across time. The lyric should resist resolution of this ambiguity. Let it stay open.

**Arrangement requirement:**
Mama D's hummed melody must be preserved in the final mix — not buried, not over-processed. It is a plot element. The music supervisor needs to know it is there; it is not a production ornament.

---

## Track 5 — *50 Ways to Leave Another* (Final Version / Full Production)

**Emotional texture:**
Not healed — present. The same emotional material as Track 1, but now inhabited rather than announced. The difference between knowing you are going to be okay and actually being okay is a production decision. This version should feel like the latter.

**Scene function:**
Final episode, final scene, final credits. This is August's lesson applied directly: whatever the budget, whatever else gets trimmed, this version of the title track is the boldest expression of the record. The production should answer the question Track 1 was asking. The strings August wished he'd budgeted for — or their equivalent — go here.

**Lyrical constraints:**
Same lyric as Track 1. Identical words. The production and the performance are what has changed. Do not rewrite the lyric to signal the arc. Trust the production.

**Arrangement requirement:**
Full orchestration — whatever the fullest version of this record sounds like, this is it. Do not compromise on this track's production scope. If budget requires a choice between this track and any other, choose this one.

## Sync-Readiness Requirements Across All Tracks

Per music supervisor expectations (CTRL Camp, Eric Campbell / sus3 Music):

| Requirement | Spec |
|---|---|
| Stereo master | WAV, 24-bit / 48kHz minimum |
| TV mix | Dialogue-safe, lead vocal ducked version |
| Stems | Full stem package per track |
| Instrumental | Clean, no lead vocal |
| A cappella | Lead vocal only, no music bed |
| Clean version | No explicit language (if applicable) |
| Metadata | Title, ISRC, ISWC, BPM, key, composer, publisher, split percentages, AI-disclosure flag |
| AI disclosure | Any AI-generated or AI-transformed element flagged with tool, version, date, and terms confirmation |

*These are delivery requirements, not aesthetic constraints. The arrangement decisions belong to the lead artist. The delivery format belongs to the project.*
