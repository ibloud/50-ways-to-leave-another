# Work-for-trade framework

_Educational concept, not an executed offer, contract, or legal/tax opinion._

A trade should exchange defined work of documented value. It should not use “exposure” as payment or make either party surrender unrelated creative rights.

## Example structure

Loptr Lab could contribute an agreed artist-site package and handoff. The artist could contribute agreed writing, performance or production services. Before work begins, both sides record:

- the cash-equivalent value and scope of each contribution;
- milestones, delivery specifications and acceptance criteria;
- whether a cash payment or reduced scope balances unequal values;
- who pays third-party costs;
- tax responsibility;
- cancellation, cure and partial-completion terms.

## Rights remain separate from value

Unless a signed agreement says otherwise:

- each party keeps all pre-existing music, recordings, code, brands, methods and materials;
- general skills, know-how and reusable tools remain with their creator;
- trade credit buys only the listed deliverables;
- songwriting and publishing shares reflect actual composition and require split sheets;
- master ownership or recording licenses must be stated separately;
- name, likeness, voice, portfolio display and promotional use require defined permission;
- exclusivity must be expressly scoped, time-limited where appropriate and included in the valuation;
- private material cannot be used for AI training without separate affirmative consent.

## Site ownership option

If the concept becomes a real Charlie-owned site, the agreement should identify what is transferred on completion (for example, final site files and Charlie-specific copy), what remains licensed (for example, reusable Loptr Lab components), and the handoff of domain, hosting, analytics, mailing list and account credentials to Charlie-controlled accounts.

## Minimum records

Use a signed trade agreement, contribution record, rights envelope, song-by-song split sheet where applicable, delivery checklist and invoice or other tax record showing the agreed value. Obtain qualified advice where the financial, copyright or tax consequences justify it.
