*Project-control documentation, not legal advice. Fees and processing times sourced from copyright.gov; re-verify before filing.*

## What you are registering and why it matters

Copyright exists automatically the moment a work is fixed in a tangible medium — a recording, a lyric document, a digital file. Registration does not create the copyright. What registration does is give you the ability to enforce it in federal court, access statutory damages (up to $150,000 per work for willful infringement), and create a public record that establishes your ownership date. A Netflix co-development conversation or sync pitch will require evidence of chain of title. Registration is that evidence.

For this EP, there are two distinct copyrights per track, each requiring separate registration:

| Copyright type | What it covers | Form | Register when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Musical composition** | Lyrics + melody as written — the song itself | Form PA (Performing Arts) | As soon as the composition exists in fixed form; before pitching |
| **Sound recording / master** | The specific recorded performance | Form SR (Sound Recording) | After the commercial master is recorded; before distribution |

You do not have a commercial master yet. That means today you register the **compositions only** (Form PA). The master registrations (Form SR) follow once recordings exist.

## Registration strategy for a 5-song EP

The Copyright Office offers a **group registration option for musical works published on the same album** — one application, one fee, up to the full album. <cite index="7-1">Registration of a claim in a group of works published on an album of music is $65.</cite>

However, your tracks are currently **unpublished**. For unpublished works, the group option also exists: <cite index="4-1">the Copyright Office has implemented a group registration option for unpublished works.</cite>

**Recommended approach:**

| Scenario | Application type | Fee | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Register all 5 compositions now (unpublished) | Group — Unpublished Works | $65 | One application covers the EP |
| Register title track composition only, immediately | Single — standard online | $65 | Use if you need the title track registered before the others are ready |
| Register title track if sole author, not-for-hire | Single — online, single author | $45 | Only if Dominique is the sole author and claimant |
| Register all 5 masters after recording | Group — Sound Recordings on an album | $65 | Separate application after masters exist |

<cite index="6-1">For a single song, by one author, with the same claimant, and not made for hire, the fee is $45. A standard application — used when your song involves multiple authors or you want to add co-writer details — costs $65.</cite>

Given that the EP has a prospective collaborator (Charlie J) whose authorship contribution is pending, **file the compositions now as a sole-author claim for Dominique's concept and lyrics**, and update the registration when co-author contributions are confirmed and the split sheet is signed. Do not list a contributor as an author until their agreement is executed.

## What you need before you file

### For the composition registration (Form PA)

- [ ] **Title of each work** — the song titles as they will appear on the EP
- [ ] **Year of creation** — the year each composition was completed in fixed form (a dated lyric document, a dated audio file, or a dated file in private storage)
- [ ] **Author information** — Dominique Devereaux; include legal name as it appears on your ID
- [ ] **Claimant information** — if Loptr Lab is an LLC, you can register under the business name as claimant; confirm with counsel whether the business or individual holds the rights
- [ ] **Nature of authorship** — "Words and music" (if both); "Words" (if lyrics only, melody TBD); be accurate
- [ ] **Publication status** — "Unpublished" if the tracks have not been publicly released or sold; this is almost certainly the correct status at this stage
- [ ] **Deposit copy** — one complete copy of the work:
  - For unpublished compositions: a lead sheet (melody + lyrics + chords), a lyric document, or a recording (the Suno demo qualifies as a deposit copy for the composition — it is evidence of the fixed form, not a claim that Suno authored the work)
  - The deposit does not become public; it is held by the Copyright Office
- [ ] **Filing fee** — credit/debit card; paid online through the eCO portal
- [ ] **eCO account** — create at copyright.gov/registration if you do not have one

### For the sound recording registration (Form SR) — file after masters are recorded

- [ ] Commercial master recordings (WAV, 24-bit/48kHz)
- [ ] Author information for the sound recording — typically the producer and/or performing artist
- [ ] Claimant — master owner (Loptr Lab, per the production agreement)
- [ ] Deposit copy — one complete copy of the sound recording per track, or the full album file
- [ ] Separate from the PA registration; submit as a new application

## Filing steps (online, via eCO portal)

1. Go to **copyright.gov/registration** and log in or create an account
2. Select **"Music, Lyrics, Sound Recordings, Scripts, Stage Plays"**
3. Choose the appropriate application type:
   - For unpublished EP compositions: **"Group of Unpublished Works"**
   - For a single composition: **"Standard Application"** or **"Single Author"** if applicable
4. Complete the application — title, author, claimant, nature of authorship, publication status
5. Upload the deposit copy (PDF of lyrics, lead sheet, or audio file as permitted)
6. Pay the filing fee by credit/debit card
7. Save the **case number** and **confirmation email** — this is your evidence of filing date while you wait for the certificate

<cite index="8-1">Electronic registration is preferred; benefits include faster processing times, status tracking, online payment, and the option to upload certain categories of works.</cite>

## Processing time and what to expect

<cite index="4-1">The Copyright Office publishes current registration processing times on its registration portal.</cite> As of early 2026, online applications for musical works typically take several months. The **registration effective date is the date the Copyright Office receives the completed application, fee, and deposit** — not the date the certificate arrives. Your filing date is what matters for enforcement purposes.

For a Netflix pitch timeline, file before you send any materials to a music supervisor or development executive. You do not need the certificate in hand — you need the filing date.

| Application | Fee |
