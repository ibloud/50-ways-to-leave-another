# Fictional capstone: The Last Bus Home

All names, works, amounts, and events are fictional. Do not place real private data in a public submission.

## Scenario

A writer-producer develops a three-song EP appearing across a three-part short series. A performer, beat maker, and editor may join. A community media center offers equipment access. No buyer, platform, or collaborator is attached.

## Assignment

1. Write a charter separating existing facts from proposals.
2. Map each song version to an episode.
3. Draft a $5,000 illustrative budget; label every number “exercise estimate.”
4. Build survival, working, and expansion cases; use $0 for unsigned licensing, grants, and acquisition.
5. Compare buying a $1,200 camera with rental and media-center access.
6. Complete fictional contribution, split, rights-envelope, and delivery templates.
7. Identify composition, master, performer, artwork, privacy, and AI questions.
8. Create a public-safe page and private evidence index.
9. Write an exit plan from a fictional video platform.
10. List five professional-review questions and five producer-controlled decisions.

Label every claim: verified within the fictional packet, proposal, estimate, interpretation, or unresolved.


## AI-assisted release decision

Use this practice in Module 6 before completing the delivery checklist. Everything below—including tools, terms, dates, recordings, and permissions—is synthetic. No audio or real account is needed. The written observation cards are the equivalent text-based path; they describe imagined listening, not a test of real systems.

### Selected reading

Read Sections **11.2–11.4** (human evaluation, listening-test design, and reporting limits) and **12**, especially its opening discussion and **12.2–12.3** (memorization, watermarking, and detection), in Modrzejewski, Kurkowska, and Herremans, [*A Tutorial on Generative Models for Music Audio*, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38252v1), submitted October 28, 2026. This is an **arXiv preprint**; see the [source record](../SOURCES.md#ai-assisted-release-reading).

Use Section 11 to ask what an evaluation actually measures and how listening conditions affect a comparison. Use Section 12 to separate training-source documentation, permission, reproduction, and output identification. An automated score, detector result, or watermark is not release permission. Broad training-source descriptions do not establish creator consent or compensation. This classroom comparison is not a controlled study or a ranking of tools.

For an actual release, check current first-party model documentation and the terms applicable to the exact tool version, account plan, inputs, and intended use. Record the source and date checked; this paper and the fictional packet cannot authorize a release.

### Same brief, two candidates

Choose a 30-second instrumental bed for the series trailer, aimed at young adult viewers. It should feel warm but uneasy, leave dialogue intelligible, build gently, and end cleanly at 30 seconds. Compare audience/mood fit, dialogue space, structure/ending, and audible artifacts. Record specific observations rather than a single quality score.

| Written observation card | Candidate A: Night Route | Candidate B: Window Seat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience and mood | Warm keys over an uneasy pulse fit the late-night scene | Bright, playful guitar feels less uneasy |
| Dialogue space | Sparse middle leaves the supplied dialogue easy to follow | Busy lead competes with dialogue between 12 and 20 seconds |
| Structure and ending | Gentle build; clean stop at 30 seconds | Gentle build; clean stop at 30 seconds |
| Artifacts | No artifacts described in this synthetic observation | A brief metallic flutter is described at 22 seconds |
