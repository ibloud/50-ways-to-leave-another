01 / READ THE CASE

A songwriter wants to understand a past publishing deal

Hypothetical scenario: A UK songwriter signed an agreement several years ago. They are unsure which compositions remain assigned, how royalty advances were recouped, and whether the agreement allows a rights reversion or a negotiated buyback. A proposed repurchase price exceeds what the songwriter can afford.

The question is not “Was this illegal?” The first task is to identify what documents and professionals are needed to assess the agreement without guessing at its terms.

What PIXIE should help organize

  1. Separate claims from evidence. Capture the artist's account, dates and source links, with unknowns clearly marked.
  2. Map the missing records. Identify the signed agreements, song schedules, royalty statements, recoupment records and any correspondence concerning rights recovery.
  3. Prepare focused referrals. Give a qualified UK music-publishing solicitor and, where appropriate, royalty specialist a short list of questions—not an AI-generated legal verdict.

PIXIE · Rights preparation brief

What we know: The songwriter reports a previous publishing agreement, uncertainty about royalty payments and an unaffordable proposed buyback.

What we cannot verify: The publisher's identity, compositions affected, ownership shares, contract terms, payments due, and whether any buyback or reversion rights exist.

Documents to locate (privately): Signed publishing agreement and amendments; schedule of compositions; royalty and recoupment statements; correspondence about repurchase; relevant society registrations.

Questions for a UK music-publishing solicitor: Which rights were assigned, for how long and in which territories? Are there accounting, audit, termination or reversion clauses? Is any money contractually outstanding? Does the proposed repurchase have any legal basis or is it a commercial negotiation?

Suggested next step: Build a private document inventory and request a qualified UK solicitor's review; use a royalty-audit specialist if statements or calculations require examination.

Do not conclude: That the deal is unlawful, that repayment automatically restores copyright, or that any named company or manager has acted improperly.

This is the intended information structure, not a verified professional referral, legal opinion or completed PIXIE capability.
